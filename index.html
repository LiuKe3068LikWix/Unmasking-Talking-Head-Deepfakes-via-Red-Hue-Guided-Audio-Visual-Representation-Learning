Unmasking Talking Head Deepfakes via Red Hue-Guided Audio-Visual Representation Learning

Ke Liu, Jiwei Wei, Yitong Qin, Quan Rui, Shiyuan He, Peng Wang, Yang Yang
University of Electronic Science and Technology of China
Paper Code Dataset
Unsupervised HSV Red Hue RFAV (NeRF / 3DGS)

Abstract

Deepfake video detection is crucial for preventing the dissemination of harmful forged audio-visual content. Although talking head deepfakes can achieve convincing synchronization between facial motion and speech, subtle discrepancies may remain in facial appearance. We observe differences in facial detail and smoothness between real and synthesized videos in their HSV red hue representations. Motivated by this observation, we propose Red Hue-based Talking Head Forgery Detection (RHTHFD), a framework that incorporates red hue information into audio-visual representation learning. Specifically, local and global features extracted by a pretrained image encoder from a temporally averaged red hue representation are used to reweight visual representations from a pretrained lip-reading network. The resulting visual representations are adaptively fused with audio features, and the detector is trained exclusively on real videos using a temporal alignment objective. We evaluate RHTHFD on public audio-visual deepfake benchmarks and the Radiance Field Audio-Visual (RFAV) dataset, which we construct using neural radiance fields and 3D Gaussian splatting. Experimental results demonstrate that RHTHFD consistently outperforms the compared baselines across these datasets. Comparisons across different hue variants further confirm the advantage of the red hue representation.

Method Overview
Open PDF
Caption. Overview of RHTHFD. The audio and visual encoders of a pretrained lip-reading network extract speech and visual features, respectively. The red hue extractor produces frame-level red hue features, which are temporally averaged before DinoV2 extracts local and global features. The V-L and V-G projectors map the visual feature to local and global branches, which are reweighted by the corresponding red hue features. The first AWG learns the relative weights of these branches, and their weighted outputs are concatenated and passed through the VLG projector. The second AWG learns the relative weights of the resulting visual feature and the speech feature before their weighted fusion is passed to the MLP predictor to produce misalignment scores. Snowflakes denote frozen encoders.

Caption. Overview of the RFAV dataset construction. Representative NeRF and 3DGS methods are selected based on visual quality, motion accuracy, lip synchronization, and head dynamics to generate high-fidelity talking head videos from portraits of multiple sources, including commonly used radiance field portraits and real-world data.
